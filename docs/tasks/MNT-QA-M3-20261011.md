## Proposed behavior and decision boundaries

For FR-100, M3 plans are linked to one vehicle and use date, odometer, or both; the first reached real target controls the occurrence. FR-101's BRD proposal to create an order on entering the alert lead window is not adopted: the 15-day/500-km `upcoming` alert does not create an occurrence or order. An `upcoming` record may exist only while no date or odometer target has been reached and no occurrence/order has been created; create the occurrence and scheduled order only when the first real target is reached.

The BRD's maintenance concepts, automatic plan generation, and recalculation include proposed or decision-required items. Maintenance alert thresholds are inherited from the SPECS §7 alert-policy bullet and are fixed boundaries, not tenant settings. These maintenance alerts are distinct from the 30-day driver-license alert preference selected in G2. Its FR-108 budget-approval proposal (approval of an estimated budget before work starts) is declined for MVP by SPECS §2.3 D14 and is outside M3. Separately, SPECS §2.3 D20 states: “Umbral opcional por empresa; si se configura, exige supervisor con incidents:approve antes de cerrar. No usar un monto arbitrario.” Apply that optional company-level threshold only to the maintenance order `completed → closed` transition: when configured, require prior approval from a supervisor with `incidents:approve` for every close, independent of the final recorded cost; when unconfigured, do not require approval. Test costs below and above the configured threshold to prove approval does not depend on final cost, without inventing a currency amount. Do not test incident closure in this MNT package. Apply the adopted SPECS §6.1 order workflow and reopen transition, including its required permission and reason; apply the explicit status suggestions in SPECS §§2.3 D11 and 6.1. Preserve traceability from proposed BRD rows to adopted decisions. Vehicle-type plans, incident-origin order linkage, and automatic vehicle-status restoration remain deferred. The cost schema/visibility contract, mechanic linkage, hour-meter use, and missing-evidence rules need traceable disposition where applicable.

Costs and parts are represented as maintenance records only. Validate amount/currency/parts detail and `view_costs` visibility at authorized boundaries; do not build inventory, stock decrement, purchasing, accounting, or financial ledger behavior. When MNT-ORDERS connects an incident, test the agreed cost-reflection seam and rollback behavior without expanding this task into incident implementation.

## Synthetic test suite

Use two synthetic tenants, A and B, with separate users, vehicles, plans, orders, mechanics, and permissions. Use deterministic identifiers, fixed clocks, and reproducible cleanup. Never use production-like identifiers or copied records.

The independent suite must cover:

- **Plans and order creation:** date and odometer windows, due/overdue boundaries, a plan scoped to one vehicle, and concurrent/retried generation. Vehicle-type plans remain deferred. A race may produce at most one next plan occurrence/order for a given plan occurrence key.
- **Order lifecycle:** valid and invalid transitions from the adopted SPECS §6.1 transition table, required completion data, cancellation reason, immutable completed records, reopen using the required permission and reason, and complete audit/history evidence.
- **Concurrent updates:** two writers attempt the same transition or close; stale writes fail consistently, and one plan occurrence is not advanced twice.
- **Vehicle blocks:** two independent active blocks can coexist for a vehicle. Completing an order may suggest a status change per the adopted contract but must not automatically restore the vehicle or clear another active block. Record the independent blocks and status suggestion in evidence.
- **Costs and parts:** synthetic labor/parts totals and currency; users with `view_costs` can read amounts, while users without it receive no cost values in API responses, exports, browser views, or error details. No inventory behavior is implied.
- **Pending evidence:** if required evidence or invoice is configured as pending/missing, completion or closure stays blocked and reports the missing evidence. Do not treat upload intent or queued storage as persisted evidence.
- **Reopen and plan idempotency:** reopening an already closed order requires the adopted permission and reason and does not create a duplicate next plan occurrence. Repeated close/reopen requests with the same idempotency key return a stable result; a conflicting payload is rejected.
- **Configured maintenance-order close review (SPECS §2.3 D20):** test only `completed → closed`. When the tenant threshold is configured, every close requires prior approval by a supervisor with `incidents:approve`, independent of final cost; exercise costs below and above the configured threshold. When no threshold is configured, no approval is required, even for a high final cost. Use the tenant's threshold and synthetic costs without inventing a currency amount. This post-completion close review is distinct from the FR-108 pre-start budget-estimate approval declined by SPECS §2.3 D14.
- **Maintenance alerts (SPECS §7):** for date and odometer plans independently, test the upcoming alert boundary at 15 calendar days or 500 km before target, no earlier alert immediately outside each boundary, due at the actual target, and overdue once the target has passed while the order remains incomplete. Each state/alert is idempotent under retries. An `upcoming` record exists only before any target is reached and before any occurrence/order is created; it does not create the occurrence/order. The occurrence and scheduled order are created only when the first real date/km target is reached. The durable in-app alert record must retain the corresponding age and threshold details. The stored outbox payload must contain only identifiers, event type, and schema version; it must not include odometer, age, threshold, or the full alert content. Do not require tenant configuration or add a grace period. The G2 30-day tenant-selected threshold applies to driver-license alerts and does not affect maintenance. Test an order no-progress signal only if MNT-ORDERS separately adopts it. Delivery transport is outside this M3 evidence boundary and does not constrain the shared MVP-NOTIFY capability.

For both alert lead boundaries, make the persistence assertions explicit:

| Given                                                                                  | When                                                                               | Then                                                                                                                                          |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| A vehicle-scoped date plan has a target 15 calendar days away and no occurrence/order. | The scan runs exactly at the 15-day upcoming boundary.                             | Persist one `upcoming` in-app alert; assert there is no occurrence and no order.                                                              |
| The same date plan has not yet reached its due date.                                   | A scan runs immediately before the actual target, then another runs at the target. | Before target, assert no occurrence and no order; at target, create exactly one occurrence and scheduled order with `due`, and no `upcoming`. |
| A vehicle-scoped odometer plan has a target 500 km away and no occurrence/order.       | The scan runs exactly at the 500-km upcoming boundary.                             | Persist one `upcoming` in-app alert; assert there is no occurrence and no order.                                                              |
| The same odometer plan has not yet reached its due odometer.                           | A scan runs immediately before the actual target, then another runs at the target. | Before target, assert no occurrence and no order; at target, create exactly one occurrence and scheduled order with `due`, and no `upcoming`. |

For dual-metric plans, apply this explicit Given/When/Then boundary matrix:

| Given                                                                                                                                          | When                                                                        | Then                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| A plan has both a due date and due odometer; its date target is reached while the odometer remains inside its 500 km upcoming window.          | The first scan evaluates the plan at the due date.                          | Emit the date-due alert and no `upcoming` alert; the due date suppresses `upcoming` even though the odometer dimension remains within its upcoming window.                                                 |

| A plan has not been scanned before and a due dimension is exactly at its target.                                                               | The first scan occurs at that exact target.                                 | Emit the due state/alert, never `upcoming`; do not synthesize an earlier upcoming record.                                                                                                                  |
| A due plan occurrence has not been scanned before and its target has already passed.                                                           | The first scan runs after the target, with no occurrence/order yet created. | Atomically create the occurrence/order and `due` record; because the target has passed and the new order remains incomplete, emit `overdue` when applicable. Never create a retroactive `upcoming` record. |

For each case, assert the durable in-app record and the minimal outbox envelope described above; retrying the scan must not duplicate records or events.

- **Tenant isolation:** tenant A cannot list, read, mutate, associate, infer, or attach tenant B mechanics, plans, vehicles, orders, files, costs, or status-block records. A foreign identifier and an unknown identifier follow the same non-disclosing error contract.
- **Permissions and ownership:** unauthorized roles cannot perform writes. An internal mechanic links to an existing same-tenant user/membership; that assigned user can access and act only on their assigned orders under SPECS D9. An external mechanic has no login and may act only through delegation; audit records the real authenticated actor. Tenant and actor come from authenticated context, not request input. Cost reads require `view_costs`.
- **Audit/outbox atomicity:** business row, status/history, audit event, and durable outbox write commit together; injected failures roll all of them back. Replays are idempotent, and no event is emitted for a rolled-back mutation.
- **Browser journey:** create a plan, observe a due order, assign a mechanic, record work/parts and evidence, complete and close it, and inspect the next occurrence. Include a second tenant isolation journey and a role without `view_costs`. Assertions must exercise the browser against the real composed API and synthetic MySQL-backed data.

Mocks and in-memory stores may support unit tests, but they are not final integration or acceptance evidence. Integration and browser evidence must exercise the actual module composition against disposable, real MySQL 8.4; record the exact server version for every run. MySQL 8.0.45 may be reported as an additional compatibility check, but it does not replace the required 8.4 run. The final M3 run may not skip or mark applicable tests pending.
